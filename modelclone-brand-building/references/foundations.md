# Foundations

Use the active user locale, owned brand facts and manual/autonomous gates in the main skill. Do not replace existing ad production quality rules.

## brand-context

Collect the owned brand name, product facts, audience, approved claims, voice, visual assets and constraints. Store source authority and gaps; avoid duplicating an existing profile.

## brand-strategy

Define the business problem, audience priority, competitive frame, promise and evidence. Distinguish an evidence-backed strategy from hypotheses requiring research.

## brand-positioning

State who the offer serves, the alternative they use, the relevant advantage and its proof. Avoid unsupported market leadership.

## target-audience

Prioritize segments using client data, pain points, buying context and objections. Casting should look sympathetic and believable, not stereotypically exhausted.

## competitor-branding

Compare actual competitor positioning, proof and visual choices from cited sources. Do not infer sales performance from visual appearance.

## Working method and deliverable

Build an evidence ledger before choosing an angle: source, date, observation, audience affected and confidence. Existing customer shares, reviews and interviews outrank a plausible invented persona. Separate the audience the business has from the audience it wishes to acquire. An age bracket alone does not explain a purchase; capture the triggering situation, desired outcome, current workaround, research behavior and reason to hesitate.

For each priority segment, produce a compact profile with life/work context, task to accomplish, frustrations, relevant values, buying trigger, objection, trusted proof and reachable channel. Include customer-language excerpts with attribution where available; otherwise label wording as a proposed paraphrase. Fictional persona names may organize findings but must not make hypothetical quotes look like research. Explain which segment is first and why using client evidence, not the agent's preference.

Test category framing in three ways: the category the company names, the alternatives customers actually compare, and a credible adjacent framing that makes the choice clearer. Map direct competitors and indirect workarounds on two purchase-relevant axes only when sources support the placements. A visually empty quadrant is not proof of demand. Record observed competitor promises and their evidence separately from your interpretation.

Write an internal positioning sentence containing audience, category, meaningful difference and reason to believe; then translate it into ordinary customer language. Check each difference: does this audience care, can the product demonstrate it, is it more than category hygiene, and could every competitor say it unchanged? Reject unsupported superiority. Finish with the selected territory, supporting facts, important exclusions and one unresolved research question that could change the recommendation.

## Handoff to production

Return: evidence ledger; prioritized segment profile; positioning decision with alternatives considered; primary pain point; product fact that addresses it; casting/environment implications; unresolved assumptions. For UGC, convert psychology into one recognizable situation and an observable action. Do not turn an older audience into tired-looking casting or invent health outcomes to strengthen an angle. A requested exact script remains locked even when this strategy suggests a different approach; propose changes separately.
