# Identity

Use the active user locale, owned brand facts and manual/autonomous gates in the main skill. Do not replace existing ad production quality rules.

## brand-identity

Prepare visual direction for type, palette, composition and photography from the owned identity. Existing genuine logos are authoritative, not regeneration targets.

## brand-naming

Explore distinct naming directions and explain pronunciation and relevance. Availability or trademark clearance requires actual independent evidence.

## brand-packaging

Brief label hierarchy, dimensions, materials and viewing distance. Real product labels, dosage tools and physical scale remain fixed in ads.

## brand-architecture

Map master brand, product lines and subbrands; explain naming hierarchy and customer comprehension before proposing changes.

## Visual identity decision brief

Translate positioning into a concrete design intention before selecting colors or references. Specify the audience, desired impression, principal use cases and existing equity to retain. Compare reference brands by the particular useful property—letterform proportions, editorial spacing, image framing—not by asking to copy the whole identity. Distinguish approved brand assets from mood references and proposed replacements.

For a new identity request, choose among wordmark, monogram, symbol-plus-name or another justified system based on name length, recognition and smallest use case. State construction character, intended feeling and category clichés to avoid. For an existing brand ad, skip new-logo ideation and use the supplied genuine mark. A stylized reference must not override its spelling or geometry.

Define color roles rather than a list of attractive swatches: primary brand color, background, body text, emphasis and supporting colors. Record exact approved values and permitted pairings; test contrast in the actual layout. Explain whether a monochromatic, neighboring-hue or contrasting scheme serves the brief. Typography needs primary/secondary roles, weights, size hierarchy, spacing, actual font availability and licensing status. Do not invent a licensed font asset or call a rasterized wordmark editable.

Describe imagery through subject, camera distance, light direction, color treatment and environmental detail. For UGC, retain plausible skin texture, sympathetic expression and lived-in context. Specify icon stroke/weight and illustration character only if the format needs them. Convert three or four design principles into observable decisions—for example, product label readable in the hero area—rather than adjectives such as premium or clean.

## Packaging and architecture checks

Packaging briefs include physical size, material, primary display panel, information hierarchy and real use context. Required label content and measured-dose tools are immutable product evidence, not decorative text. Show the product at the scale it will appear in the ad and list prohibited changes. Architecture work maps parent brand, subbrand, product line and variant naming; explain whether the customer needs a shared family resemblance or distinct positioning, and document exceptions.

Deliver a designer-ready brief with asset IDs, color/type specifications, imagery direction, exclusions and application notes for requested touchpoints. Inspect an actual application before calling the direction validated. A mood board alone cannot establish label fidelity, mobile readability or realistic object scale.
