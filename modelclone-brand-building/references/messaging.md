# Messaging

Use the active user locale, owned brand facts and manual/autonomous gates in the main skill. Do not replace existing ad production quality rules.

## brand-messaging

Arrange a primary promise, audience-specific benefits, substantiating facts and a concrete CTA. Keep claims traceable to supplied evidence.

## brand-voice

Derive tone, sentence rhythm, vocabulary and exclusions from approved samples. Show on-brand/off-brand contrasts without copying another author’s identity.

## brand-story

Build an origin narrative from verified events and founder statements; leave unknown milestones blank rather than inventing them.

## brand-manifesto

Express a specific belief and observable commitments. Avoid abstract grandiosity and claims the brand cannot demonstrate.

## personal-brand

Define the actual person’s expertise, viewpoint, evidence and content boundaries. Do not invent credentials or personal experience.

## Build a message system

Start with the central customer benefit and the factual mechanism that supports it. Write a strategic core message and a short value proposition answering what the product is, whom it helps, what changes and why this offer is relevant. This foundation is not automatically the final headline. Keep a proof bank with the exact fact, source, allowed wording and limits; ingredient presence does not prove comparative clinical performance.

Arrange the public message as headline, supporting explanation, a small set of benefit pillars and proof under each. A pillar without supporting evidence is a hypothesis, not approved copy. Adapt the lead for each audience: what matters most, what they already believe, their likely objection, the most useful proof and language to avoid. Preserve the central proposition while changing length and emphasis for a social bio, product page, ad or sales presentation.

If taglines are requested, compare a few genuinely different directions—functional, emotional or lightly playful—against clarity, specificity, memorability and truth. Include an existing approved slogan in the assessment instead of replacing it by default. Do not impose a fixed number of alternatives or squeeze a long brand name into an arbitrary word count. Explain the recommended choice in terms of the brief.

## Voice and narrative methods

Describe voice with usable pairs and examples: warm without exaggerated intimacy; informed without jargon; concise without clipped fragments. Record sentence rhythm, vocabulary, terminology, capitalization and words the brand avoids from supplied samples. Tone may vary with situation while voice stays recognizable. Use the user's locale and spoken conventions; do not import an upstream writer's punctuation rules or British English.

For an origin story, build a factual sequence: initial problem, decisive observation, action taken and present commitment. Separate founder quotations from proposed narration. A manifesto should express a belief through actions the company takes, not unsupported claims about changing the world. Personal-brand material needs real expertise and actual examples, not an invented biography.

## Copy handoff and checks

Deliver core message, hierarchy, audience adaptations, proof bank, voice rules and channel-ready examples only for requested channels. Read speech aloud and check that the opening names a recognizable pain point the product can credibly address. Every spoken tense must fit the planned scene; each promised benefit needs an appropriate fact or demonstration. Keep scene directions separate from speech. Test the CTA: can the viewer identify the next action and does the destination actually support it? Flag unsupported statements separately; preserve explicitly locked client scripts pending an authorized change.
