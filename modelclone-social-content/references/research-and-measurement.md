# Research And Measurement

Use the active user locale, owned brand facts and manual/autonomous gates in the main skill. Do not replace existing ad production quality rules.

## post-scorer

Provide editorial assessment with explicit criteria; compare performance only against supplied or actually retrieved history. Never invent predicted engagement scores.

## content-matrix

Map audience needs against topics, formats and funnel intent. Each proposed asset needs a distinct purpose; avoid repetitive filler.

## niche-research

Identify questions, objections and themes from accessible cited sources. Separate observed patterns from hypotheses and disclose inaccessible feeds.

## analytics-dashboard

Use supplied exports or supported owned data. Define periods, denominators and missing values; report measured outcomes rather than fabricated charts.

## Research and matrix construction

Identify the audience question before collecting sources. Group evidence into recurring problems, desired outcomes, objections, terminology and purchase/usage context. Cite accessible source material and distinguish recurring observations from a single anecdote. Use supplied exports or supported authorized access; no automatic scraping, comment collection or borrowed account credentials.

Build a content matrix crossing audience need with format and intent. Each cell should name an actual angle, evidence needed, likely takeaway and possible asset. Use the matrix to find useful gaps rather than filling every square. A repeated topic needs a different question or proof, not a superficial rewrite. Mark unvalidated ideas as hypotheses and prioritize by audience relevance and production feasibility.

## Historical review versus editorial critique

Before using post history, verify author, collection period, sample size, included formats and missing data. Missing metrics are unavailable, not zero. Choose a meaningful engagement definition and disclose it; a weighted reaction/comment index is an editorial proxy, not reach or business impact. Compare high and low performers only when the sample is sufficiently broad and comparable, noting selection and age effects.

Look for patterns in opening type, topic, length, format, CTA and sentence rhythm. A top-decile comparison can help with a large enough sample but is not a universal requirement. Do not infer causality from a hook appearing in successful posts. Record the observed pattern and the draft feature it may inform; keep correlation and interpretation distinct.

Review a draft for hook specificity, voice fit, useful information, structure/mobile readability and factual readiness. Each finding needs the exact passage and a concrete suggested change. Voice fit is unavailable without samples; historical fit is unavailable without history. Numeric ratings are optional summaries of stated editorial criteria, never predictions of reach. Do not fabricate a denominator or score missing dimensions as failures.

## Deliverable and decision

Return data source, period, sample/comparability limits, measured patterns, editorial findings and the few highest-impact edits. Where actual analytics exist, show definitions and calculations behind a dashboard and retain unknowns. For a test recommendation, state the variable being changed, what remains comparable, the metric and the decision a result would support. Reviewing content does not publish it, alter tracking or establish a working analytics integration.
