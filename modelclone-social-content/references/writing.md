## voice-builder

Use the user’s own samples to identify rhythm, vocabulary, tone and boundaries. Without samples, label the voice provisional; never adopt the upstream sample author’s life or metrics.

## newsletter-voice

Adapt the approved voice to a useful recurring structure and reader promise. Examples and numbers must be real; generic archetypes are optional scaffolds.

## hook-generator

Offer distinct openings grounded in a relevant pain point, observation or verified fact. Deliver on the hook in the body; avoid invented urgency or sensational guarantees.

## post-writer

Write one coherent point with a concrete example, useful takeaway and suitable next step. Preserve supplied facts and the user’s locale.

## post-formatter

Improve mobile readability and logical order without forcing every post into a formula, fixed line count or unnatural sentence breaks.

## reels-scripting

## quote-post

Write original attributable text or accurately quote a supplied source. Do not invent quotations from real people; inspect type contrast and attribution.

## Voice, hook and structure choices

Derive voice from supplied author samples: sentence length, paragraph rhythm, first/second-person balance, vocabulary, stance and recurring omissions. Separate stylistic observations from biographical facts. A provisional voice without samples should be labeled as such. Newsletter work can use a teaching sequence, analysis, story or practical roundup according to reader intent; it does not require invented numbers or the upstream author's archetype.

Build hooks from different evidence-supported angles: a specific observation or number, a defensible counterpoint, a real transformation, a relevant authority reference, an actual admission, or a clearly uncertain future implication. Choose only angles the source supports. Personal openings require supplied experience; numbers require a source. Tension should make the topic worth reading and the body must resolve it. Do not force clickbait, a digit, a two-line contrast, a 40-character limit or a ban on questions when the brief calls for natural speech.

Choose a structure that fits the material: problem→consequence→solution for a real pain point; before→after→bridge for a demonstrated change; situation→action→result for a sourced story; or attention→interest→reason→action for explanatory persuasion. Treat frameworks as drafting options, not mandatory labels in the finished copy. Keep one main idea and a useful takeaway. Format for mobile without turning every clause into an isolated dramatic line.

## Reels analysis and production handoff

When an actual reference video is available, capture the opening words, approximate hook length, section timing, visual transitions, language rhythm, turning point and CTA. Distinguish observed technique from assumed performance. A popular-looking Reel is not a verified outlier without a comparable creator baseline. Supplied transcript alone supports verbal analysis, not claims about camera movement or product handling.

Transfer the useful technique to original material rather than copying the reference's claims or mechanically matching its word count. Produce separate fields for exact speech, visible action, shot/angle, required reference assets and intended transition. Use as few points as fit the measured audio and the user's brief. The hook should establish relevance promptly; physical actions must be achievable in sequence. A comment-to-receive CTA requires a real resource and working authorized delivery route; otherwise use an honest next step.

For ModelClone ads, generate and verify original speech first, split at natural phrase boundaries, and prepare each shot's references before motion. Do not replace the established copy/direction skills with generic Reel templates. Keep captions aligned with locked speech and actual timestamps; do not put subtitles over the final card. A copy draft is not evidence of audio fidelity or a finished video.
